# Product name brainstorm

## Selected name and icon — September 2, 2026

The app is **Yum Chase**, with a space, as explicitly selected by Chase. This
overrides the earlier one-word CamelCase preference and the VitaChase trial.
The selected icon is **Y1 Peach Mochi**, the first YumChase concept from
[round 2](icon-concepts/2026-09-02-round-2/README.md).

![Selected Peach Mochi app icon](icon-concepts/2026-09-02-round-2/yum-chase-01-peach-mochi.png)

The Home Screen name, diary title, widgets, Siri responses, onboarding, import
instructions, and system permission copy use Yum Chase. The approved opaque
1024×1024 PNG is copied unchanged into the primary AppIcon asset; iOS supplies
the rounded-corner mask.

The Xcode project, targets, executable, source folders, bundle identifiers, App
Group, widget kinds, existing `vitachase://` links, storage directories, HealthKit
identifiers, and the `.calmeal` import format remains unchanged. This is a branding
update, not a data migration or a new app installation. Physical-device deployment
is a separate step; update the existing app without uninstalling it.

Availability screening, App Store reservation, and formal trademark clearance
have not been performed for Yum Chase. Selection is not a claim of availability.

## Product naming brief

The product is a fast, local-first food diary for someone who wants to understand
whether their eating matches a small set of personally important health
priorities. Calories and macros remain useful, but they are only part of the
picture. Optional focus areas include alcohol, fiber, processed meat, and red
meat, with room for additional signals later.

The name should therefore:

- cover food quality and health priorities beyond calories or weight loss;
- feel focused, fast, and confident without promising medical outcomes;
- be memorable and natural in “Open ___” and “I logged it in ___”;
- remain legible beneath an iPhone app icon;
- support the portfolio's `Chase` suffix, with the approved two-word spelling
  **Yum Chase**;
- avoid sounding derivative of an established calorie-tracking app.

The crowded category vocabulary includes *calorie*, *cal*, *macro*, *food*,
*fit*, *my*, *pal*, *diary*, *coach*, *AI*, *snap*, and *bite*. Combining one
of those words with `Chase` would likely feel descriptive, keyword-driven, or
too close to an established competitor.

The strongest names generally commit to one memorable frame: an outcome such as
Lose It!, an instrument such as Cronometer, a coined brand such as Lifesum or
Yazio, or a specific system such as MacroFactor. The subtitle and App Store
description can carry the category explanation.

## Earlier recommendation — August 28, 2026: VitaChase

**VitaChase** uses *vita*, meaning life, as a compact bridge between nutrition,
vitality, and long-term health. It does not reduce the product to calories,
macros, fitness, or weight loss, and it can encompass both positive targets such
as fiber and exposures someone may want to limit, such as alcohol or processed
meat.

It works in ordinary speech:

- Open VitaChase.
- I logged it in VitaChase.
- Download VitaChase.

Suggested presentation:

- **App Store name:** VitaChase
- **Subtitle:** Private food & health tracker
- **Positioning:** The fast, private food log for tracking what matters to your
  health.

Tradeoffs:

- *Vita* has a mild vitamin or supplement association.
- Pronunciation may split between “VEE-ta” and “VY-ta.”
- The name does not explain food tracking without its subtitle or surrounding
  presentation.

### Preliminary public screening

Signals observed on **August 28, 2026**:

- No exact public VitaChase result appeared in the U.S. App Store catalog.
- No obvious nutrition, health, or software product using the exact compound
  appeared in broader web searches; old unrelated art and hashtag usage did
  appear.
- `vitachase.com` had no Verisign registry record at the time of checking.
- App Store Connect reservation and formal trademark clearance have not been
  performed.

These are preliminary, time-sensitive signals rather than a conclusion that the
name is available or legally safe.

## Other directions considered

| Candidate | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **MacroChase** | Clear portfolio fit, but overweights calories and macros relative to the product’s broader health-focus model. |
| **SignalChase** | Strong product metaphor for personally selected health signals, but `signalchase.com` was registered in July 2025 and “Signal” has substantial unrelated software search noise. |
| **IntentChase** | Captures intentional, user-selected priorities and had encouraging preliminary public signals, but provides little nutrition context. |
| **HealthspanChase** | Expresses the long-term outcome well, but *healthspan* is already crowded across apps, wearables, nutrition products, and supplement businesses. |
| **FoodSignalChase** | More descriptive than SignalChase, but long and mechanically assembled. |
| **Dialed** | The earlier standalone recommendation; energetic and relevant to configurable focus areas, but it does not participate in the Chase portfolio convention. |

Earlier ideas such as BalanceChase, NourishChase, HabitChase, PlateChase,
ChoiceChase, HealthChase, BiteChase, VitalityChase, FuelChase, and WholeChase
were understandable but felt generic or weak in comparison with established
category brands.

## Naming guardrails

- Use **Yum Chase**, including the space, for all current product presentation.
- Keep the earlier CamelCase spellings only when discussing historical candidates.
- Avoid `Chaser` and avoid leading with Chase.
- Avoid names centered only on calories when selectable health focus areas are
  a core differentiator.
- Avoid medical, diagnostic, or moralizing language.
- Do not add Chase to another product’s distinctive name.
- Repeat App Store, Google Play, web, domain, and trademark screening before a
  final decision.
- Treat App Store reservation, domain registration, and trademark clearance as
  separate steps.
